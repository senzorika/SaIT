The panel must be calibrated

In descriptive profiling the panel replaces a laboratory instrument. It must meet three conditions at once:

pillar question ANOVA effect
discrimination does it separate products? Product – we want a small p
agreement does he/she rate like the others? Product:Panelist – we want a large p
repeatability the same when repeated? Session, residual – small variance

Dataset sensochoc: 29 assessors, 6 chocolates, 2 sessions, 14 descriptors.

Agreement and repeatability of assessors

avg <- with(sensochoc, tapply(CocoaA, list(Product, Panelist), mean))
agreement <- apply(avg, 2, cor, y = rowMeans(avg))
s1 <- with(subset(sensochoc, Session == 1),
           tapply(CocoaA, list(Product, Panelist), mean))
s2 <- with(subset(sensochoc, Session == 2),
           tapply(CocoaA, list(Product, Panelist), mean))
rep_ <- sapply(colnames(s1), function(h) cor(s1[, h], s2[, h]))
weak <- agreement < 0.5 & rep_ < 0.5
plot(agreement, rep_, pch = 19, cex = 1.4,
     col = ifelse(weak, pal[2], pal[3]),
     xlim = c(-1, 1), ylim = c(-1, 1),
     xlab = "agreement with the panel", ylab = "repeatability")
abline(h = 0.5, v = 0.5, lty = 2)

The orange assessors (7) are below the limit on both indicators – candidates for retraining.

QUIZ What to do with the assessor?

An assessor has high repeatability but low agreement with the panel. What does it mean?

He/she rates consistently, but “in their own way” – probably understands the descriptor differently. Solution: calibration with reference standards, not removal.

Fixed vs. random effects

We want “A is better than B” for any similar panel, not just our 29 people → the product is tested against assessor disagreement (the interaction).

Variance decomposition

vc <- as.data.frame(VarCorr(m))
share <- setNames(vc$vcov / sum(vc$vcov),
                  c("interaction", "assessor", "residual"))
barplot(as.matrix(share), horiz = TRUE,
        col = pal[c(2, 3, 1)], border = NA,
        xlab = "share of variance")
round(share, 2)
interaction    assessor    residual 
       0.09        0.19        0.72

A large interaction = poor agreement. A large residual = poor repeatability. The bridge between panel performance and the model.

Post-hoc in a mixed model

comp <- emmeans(m, pairwise ~ Product, adjust = "tukey")
plot(comp$emmeans) + ggplot2::labs(x = "estimated mean of Bitterness", y = NULL)

QUIZ Singular fit

For CocoaA, lmer prints the warning “boundary (singular) fit”. What happened?

The variance of the assessor × product interaction is estimated as 0 (interaction p = 0.99) – assessors agree on CocoaA. The model is usable; the effect can be dropped.

Summary

  • Check the panel regularly: discrimination, agreement, repeatability.
  • Weak assessor → calibration first, removal only at the end.
  • The assessor is a random effect → lmer(y ~ Product + (1 | Panelist) + (1 | Panelist:Product)).
  • Post-hoc with emmeans, not TukeyHSD.
