Comparing several samples

Kruskal-Wallis, Friedman, analysis of variance (ANOVA) and multiple comparisons.

Script (SK): cvicenie5b.txtScript (EN): exercise5b.txt

What you will learn

  • to compare three or more samples at once instead of a series of t-tests,
  • to choose between ANOVA, the Kruskal-Wallis and the Friedman test,
  • to find out with a post-hoc test which samples differ.

Why not several t-tests?

Three samples give three pairs, five samples already ten. Each test has a 5% risk of a false alarm, so with 10 tests the chance of at least one false “difference” is about 40%. That is why we first run one global test (are there any differences?) and only when it is significant, post-hoc comparisons with a correction.

P(at least 1 error) = 1 − (1 − 0.05)m   →   m = 10: 1 − 0.9510 ≈ 0.40

ANOVA – decomposing variability

Analysis of variance compares how much the group means differ from each other (variability between) relative to the spread of values within groups. If groups are far apart and compact inside, F is large.

F = [SSbetween / (k − 1)] / [SSwithin / (N − k)] k – number of groups, N – number of all ratings. Large F → small p → at least one group differs.

Kruskal-Wallis test – ANOVA on ranks

If the data are not normal, we replace the values by their ranks in the pooled set (smallest = 1, largest = N, ties get the average rank) and compare the mean ranks of the groups. Outliers (e.g. the rating of 1 for product C) then do not have an undue influence.

Mind the order in the formulaThe correct form is kruskal.test(value ~ group) – the measured variable on the left, the factor on the right. The reversed kruskal.test(group ~ value) tests something completely different and gives a meaningless result.

Friedman test – blocks of assessors

In a ranking test every assessor ranks all samples (1 = best). The assessor is a block: everyone may use the scale differently, so ranks are compared within a row and only then summed.

friedman.test(taste ~ product | assessor)             # value ~ group | block
frdAllPairsNemenyiTest(taste, product, assessor)      # PMCMRplus

Post-hoc comparisons

  • Tukey HSD after ANOVA – compares all pairs of means and returns adjusted p-values and confidence intervals of the differences (diff, lwr, upr). If the interval does not contain 0, the difference is significant.
  • Bonferroni correction – multiplies every p-value by the number of comparisons (p.adj = "bonferroni"). Simple but conservative.
  • Nemenyi test after Friedman – compares rank sums.
Two-way ANOVAaov(taste ~ gender * product) tests three things: the effect of product, the effect of gender and their interaction – whether products are liked differently by men and women. A significant interaction means the main effects cannot be interpreted on their own.
