EXERCISE 2

BCG matrix and confidence interval

How to place products on a price × quality map and how to express the uncertainty of a panel mean.

Script (SK): cvicenie2.txtScript (EN): exercise2.txt

What you will learn

  • to build a modified price × quality BCG matrix and interpret its quadrants,
  • to compute and plot the 95% confidence interval of a mean,
  • why a small panel needs the t-distribution instead of the number 1.96.

Modified BCG matrix

The original Boston Consulting Group matrix sorts products by market growth and market share. In sensory analysis we modify it: the x-axis shows price, the y-axis sensory quality (panel mean). The means of both variables split the plane into four quadrants – each product is compared with the “average product” on the market.

Why the mean and not “by eye”?The dividing lines should be computed (abline(v = mean(price))), not typed in by hand. When a new product is added the mean changes, and hard-coded numbers would put products in the wrong quadrant.

Confidence interval of the mean

The panel mean is only an estimate of the true rating of the product. If 7 different people had rated it, we would get a slightly different number. A confidence interval (CI) gives the range in which the true mean lies with a given certainty (usually 95%).

CI95% = x̄ ± t0.975; n−1 · s / √n x̄ – mean, s – standard deviation, n – number of assessors, s/√n – standard error of the mean (SE)

1.96 or t?

The number 1.96 holds for the normal distribution, i.e. for large samples. With a small panel we do not know the true variance, only its estimate s, so we use Student’s t-distribution, which has “heavier tails” and gives a wider interval:
