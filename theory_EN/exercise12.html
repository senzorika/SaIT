What you will learn

The JAR scale

A hedonic scale tells us whether the product is liked. A JAR scale tells us what to do with it. The consumer judges the intensity of each attribute relative to their ideal:

The centre of the scale is the optimum, not the “average” – that is the main difference from an intensity scale. A good product has most answers in the centre; asymmetry shows the direction for adjusting the recipe.

Diverging stacked bar chart

JAR shareInterpretation (rule of thumb)
≥ 70%the attribute is fine, do not change it
50 – 70%monitor; if one side is clearly stronger (> 20%), consider adjusting
< 50%adjust the attribute in the direction of the dominant side

Penalty analysis

A JAR scale tells us how many consumers find an attribute too weak or too strong. It does not tell us whether that really bothers them. Penalty analysis therefore combines JAR with overall liking: for each “too little” and “too much” group it computes how much lower the group rates the product than the “just about right” group.

mean drop = x̄JAR − x̄group  weighted drop = mean drop · share of the group x̄ – mean liking on the hedonic scale; share of the group as a decimal number

The rules are conventionsGroups below 20 % are usually not interpreted (here “thickness – too much” is only 3 consumers, hence the meaningless negative drop). A drop of 1 point is often taken as worth acting on and 0.5 point as borderline – this is not a standard, set your limits in advance. A ready-made solution is also offered by the SensoMineR package (function JAR()).

Radar chart (profile chart)

A radar (spider) chart shows the sensory profile: each axis is one descriptor, the distance from the centre is the mean intensity. The shape of the polygon is the “fingerprint” of the product – similar products have similar shapes.

Watch outThe area of the polygon depends on the order of the axes – the same data with a different descriptor order look different. The chart suits 3 – 5 products and 5 – 12 descriptors; with more it becomes cluttered. radarchart() requires the maximum and minimum of the axes in the first two rows of the data – hence rbind(rep(9, 5), rep(0, 5), data).

Task solutions

Click to expand a solution. Try the task on your own first – results were verified in R 4.5. The code continues from the variables in the script.

Task 1 – which attribute to adjust first 
penalty[order(-penalty$weighted_drop), ]

Two groups pass the 20 % rule and show a large drop: “thickness – too little” (57 % of consumers, drop 0.96 points, weighted drop 0.54) and “sweetness – too much” (28 %, drop 1.79 points, weighted drop 0.51). The yogurt should be made thicker and less sweet; thickness affects more people, sweetness bothers them more strongly (t-test: p < 0.001 and p = 0.003). Sourness needs no change – the drop is below 0.1 points. The groups “sweetness – too little” (18 %), “sourness – too much” (10 %) and “thickness – too much” (5 %) fail the 20 % rule.
