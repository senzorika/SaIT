TURF – Total Unduplicated Reach and Frequency

A pizzeria can offer only a limited number of ingredients. It does not want to pick the three most popular ones – they may appeal to the same people. It wants three that together reach as many different customers as possible.

IndicatorDefinitionQuestion
Reachshare of respondents who like at least one item of the combinationHow many do we reach?
Frequencymean number of liked items of the combination per respondentHow strongly do we reach them?

The data are binary: 1 = the ingredient is attractive for the respondent, 0 = it is not. The function turf(pizza, 11, 3:4) goes through all combinations of 3 and 4 items out of 11 (165 and 330 combinations) and sorts them by reach. The column wgt is the respondent weight (with real data, e.g. for sample representativeness).

Mind the random dataIn the script the answers are generated with sample(), so the results differ on every run and all combinations have a similar reach (~87 – 94%). For a meaningful result use a real questionnaire, and set.seed() when simulating.

Preference map (external preference mapping)

We have two data sources about the same 16 products:

  • the sensory profile from a trained panel – what the product is like (13 descriptors),
  • the hedonic rating from 100 consumers – how much they like it.

The preference map connects them: from the profile we make a PCA map of products, and for every consumer we project into it the region of the map where their rating would be highest. The function carto() from SensoMineR computes, for every point of the map, the share of consumers whose preference exceeds their own mean there, and draws it as contour lines.

sort(rowMeans(hedonic), decreasing = TRUE)     # mean rating of the products
res.pca <- PCA(sensory)
carto(res.pca$ind$coord[, 1:2], hedonic)

Our product 1 has a mean of 2.99 and ranks only 8th of 16; the best rated are 16 (3.44), 3 (3.36) and 13 (3.16). On the carto() map look at the zone product 1 lies in and which descriptors (PCA arrows) point to the zone with the highest share of satisfied consumers – those should be strengthened. Note: the PCA map explains only 39% of the profile variability (22 + 17%), so interpret it with care.
