EXERCISE 15

Sensory panel performance

Discrimination, agreement and repeatability – how to check that the panel and each assessor can be trusted.

Script (SK): cvicenie15.RScript (EN): exercise15.R🎓 Slides

What you will learn

  • the three pillars of panel performance: discrimination, agreement and repeatability,
  • to read the p-value tables of panelperf() and paneliperf(),
  • to find assessors who need retraining.

A sensory panel is a measuring instrument

In descriptive profiling the panel replaces a laboratory instrument. Like an instrument, it must be regularly calibrated and checked. The dataset sensochoc (SensoMineR) contains ratings of 6 chocolates by 29 assessors in 2 sessions on 14 descriptors.

Performance of the whole panel – panelperf()

For every descriptor an ANOVA is computed with the effects of product, assessor, session and their interactions. coltable() colours the table of p-values:

The Panelist effect is almost always significantAssessors use different parts of the scale (one rates 3 – 6, another 5 – 9). That alone is fine as long as the product order stays the same. The problem is the Product:Panelist interaction.

Individual assessors

The function paneliperf() computes the same indicators for each assessor. The script also computes them “by hand” for one descriptor:

  • agreement = correlation of the assessor’s product means with the panel means,
  • repeatability = correlation between session 1 and session 2 of the same assessor.
Remove an assessor only as a last resortA poor result on one descriptor is usually caused by an unclear attribute definition, not by the assessor. First check whether others have the same problem, and clarify the definition and reference standards.

Task solutions

Task 1 – best discriminated descriptors and worst agreement 
descriptors <- names(sensochoc)[5:18]
result <- t(sapply(descriptors, function(v) {
  a <- summary(aov(as.formula(paste(v, "~ Product + Panelist + Session + Product:Panelist")),
                   data = sensochoc))[[1]]
  rownames(a) <- trimws(rownames(a))
  c(F_product = a["Product", "F value"], p_interaction = a["Product:Panelist", "Pr(>F)"])
}))
result[order(-result[, "F_product"]), ]

All product p-values are practically 0, so we compare F. Best discriminated: MilkF (F = 110), CocoaF (68), Bitterness (63). Worst agreement (smallest interaction p): Melting (0.0002), Acidity (0.005), Vanilla (0.009).

Task 2 – leaving out weak assessors 
weak <- names(agreement)[agreement < 0.5 & repeatability[names(agreement)] < 0.5]
weak
reduced <- droplevels(sensochoc[!sensochoc$Panelist %in% weak, ])
summary(aov(CocoaA ~ Product + Panelist, data = sensochoc))
summary(aov(CocoaA ~ Product + Panelist, data = reduced))

Both below 0.5: 7 assessors (5, 6, 7, 11, 23, 25, 26). Without them the product F rises from 14.6 to 19.8 even though there are fewer assessors – panel discrimination improves. Before removing anyone, check the other descriptors too.
