EXERCISE 10

Survival analysis and sensory shelf life

Kaplan-Meier estimate of the probability of product acceptance over time and finding the cut-off point.

Script (SK): cvicenie10.RScript (EN): exercise10.R🎓 Slides

What you will learn

  • to frame sensory shelf life as a survival analysis problem,
  • to build and read a Kaplan-Meier curve,
  • to estimate the cut-off time at which half of the consumers reject the product,
  • to estimate shelf life from interval-censored data with a Weibull model and convert it to another temperature (Q10, Arrhenius).

Shelf life from the consumer’s point of view

Microbiological shelf life tells us when a product becomes unsafe. Sensory shelf life tells us when the consumer rejects it – odour, taste or texture changes. The observed event is therefore rejection, and the question is: what is the probability that a product stored for t hours will still be accepted?

The method comes from medicine (patient survival), hence the name “survival analysis”. It fits here because it works with censored data – ratings where rejection did not happen and the exact rejection time is unknown.

Kaplan-Meier estimate

At every time when at least one rejection occurs, the probability of acceptance is multiplied by the share of those who “survived”:

Ŝ(t) = ∏ti ≤ t (1 − di / ni) di – number of rejections at time ti, ni – number of ratings “at risk” (not yet rejected or censored) just before ti

Cut-off point with regression

The second part of the script fits a line through the points (time, Ŝ) and looks for the time at which Ŝ = 0.5. Since we want time from probability, the model is reversed: lm(x ~ y), i.e. time = a · Ŝ + b. The function model(0.5) then returns the estimated cut-off time.

tcut-off = a · 0.5 + b
Which limit to choose?50% rejections is a common choice (the median), but a producer may require a stricter limit, e.g. 25% rejections (Ŝ = 0.75). Just call model(0.75).
Coding the eventIn Surv(time, event), TRUE must mean rejection. If you coded acceptance, the curve would have the opposite meaning. The script data contain TRUE even at 0 h – in a real experiment check whether someone really rejected the fresh product or whether it is a coding error.

Interval-censored data

The first example treats each of the 35 ratings as a separate observation with an exact event time. In a consumer shelf-life study, however, every consumer tastes samples of all storage times and the exact time at which they would first reject the product is unknown. We only know between which two times it happened (Hough, 2010):

intervals <- Surv(lower, upper, type = "interval2")
weibull <- survreg(intervals ~ 1, dist = "weibull")

The Weibull model

With interval data the rejection time is described by a parametric distribution, most often the Weibull. It gives the time for any share of rejections together with a confidence interval.

S(t) = exp(−(t / η)β) S(t) – share of consumers who still accept the product at time t; η – scale parameter, β – shape parameter (β > 1: the risk of rejection grows with time). In survreg: η = exp(Intercept), β = 1 / Scale.

Kaplan-Meier or Weibull?The Kaplan-Meier curve is a quick first look at the data. For estimating a shelf life with a confidence interval, a parametric model on interval-censored data is recommended in sensory shelf-life work (Hough, 2010; ISO 16779).

Accelerated storage: Q10 and Arrhenius

For products with a long shelf life the test is accelerated by a higher temperature. The factor Q10 says how many times the shelf life shortens when the temperature rises by 10 °C:

Q10 = (tT₁ / tT₂)10 / (T₂ − T₁)  ln k = ln A − Ea / (R · T) t – shelf life at temperature T; k ~ 1/t – rate of change; Ea – activation energy; R = 8.314 J/(mol·K); T in kelvin
Temperature5 °C15 °C25 °C8 °C (prediction)
shelf life (days)2813622.1

The script data give Q10 = 2.16 and Ea = 53.1 kJ/mol.

Beware of extrapolationThe Arrhenius model assumes that the same spoilage mechanism operates at all temperatures. At a high temperature it may change (fat melting, a different microflora) – a prediction outside the measured range has to be verified by storage at the real temperature.

The time at which 25 % of consumers reject the product is 14.7 h (95 % CI 9.9 – 19.6 h). The median is 25.6 h – by then half of the consumers would already reject the product. A cautious producer chooses the lower limit of the interval, i.e. about 10 h.
