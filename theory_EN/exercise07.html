Principal component analysis (PCA)

When we rate many attributes at once, many of them are related (sweetness and fruitiness, price and brand…). PCA rotates the coordinate system so that the first axis – the 1st principal component – explains as much of the variability in the data as possible, the second (perpendicular to the first) as much of the rest as possible, and so on. Instead of 4, 10 or 20 attributes, 2 – 3 components are then enough and the data can be shown in a plane.

Because the attributes have different units and ranges, PCA is computed from the correlation matrix (princomp(data, cor = TRUE)) – every attribute then has the same weight.

Biplot

A biplot draws both the objects (respondents or products, points) and the variables (arrows). Arrows close together = positively correlated attributes; opposite direction = negative correlation; perpendicular = unrelated. A point lying in the direction of an arrow has a high value of that attribute.

Factor analysis (FA)

PCA is descriptive – it only rearranges the variability. Factor analysis assumes a model: the observed answers are caused by a few unmeasurable (latent) factors plus individual noise. The aim is to reveal these factors and name them.

For the data of the second example the eigenvalues of the correlation matrix are 2.46; 1.69; 0.89; 0.61; 0.27; 0.08 – two are greater than 1, hence factanal(data, factors = 2).

factanal outputMeaning

p-value at the endtest “are k factors enough?” – p > 0.05 means yes
Varimax rotationBy default factanal rotates the factors with varimax so that each attribute has a high loading on only one factor – which makes the factors easier to name. In the perfume task, name the factors after the scents that make them up (e.g. “floral” vs. “woody”).
