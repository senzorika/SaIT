EXERCISE 8

Cluster analysis

Hierarchical clustering (Ward), the dendrogram and k-means for product segmentation.

Script (SK): cvicenie8.RScript (EN): exercise8.R🎓 Slides

What you will learn

  • to measure product similarity as a distance in the space of sensory attributes,
  • to build and read the dendrogram of hierarchical clustering (Ward’s method),
  • to split products with k-means and choose the optimal number of clusters.

Similarity = small distance

Each product is a point in a space whose coordinates are the mean intensities of the descriptors (saltiness, sourness, crispness…). Two products are similar if their points are close. Most often we use the Euclidean distance:

d(A, B) = √[ Σj (xAj − xBj)² ] j – descriptors. If attributes use different scales, standardise the data first: scale(sensory).

Hierarchical clustering

The algorithm starts with every product as its own cluster and in each step merges the two closest clusters until only one remains. The merging history is shown by the dendrogram. Ward’s method merges the clusters whose union increases the within-cluster variability the least – it creates compact groups of similar size.

Reading a dendrogramSimilarity is given by the merge height, not by how close the labels are on the x-axis. Products 12 and 3 are side by side, yet they join only at the top of the tree – they are very dissimilar.

Optimal number of clusters – silhouette

The silhouette coefficient of each product compares the mean distance to its own cluster (a) with the distance to the nearest other cluster (b): s = (b − a) / max(a, b). Values close to 1 = the product is well placed, around 0 = it lies on the border, negative = probably misplaced. fviz_nbclust(…, method = "silhouette") plots the mean silhouette for k = 1 … 10; the optimum is the maximum of the curve.

Example – which product is most similar to our A? 
sensory <- read.table("http://senzorika.com/sait/datasety/senzorika.txt", sep = ",")
row.names(sensory) <- c("A","B","C","D","E","F","G","H","I","J","K","L","M","N","O","X")
fit <- hclust(dist(sensory), method = "ward.D")
groups <- cutree(fit, k = 5)
groups[groups == groups["A"]]
sort(as.matrix(dist(sensory))["A", ])[2:4]

With 5 groups our product A is in a group with I and X. The closest one is X (Euclidean distance 2.00), then G (2.15) and I (2.52). G is close, yet Ward placed it in another group – distance to one product and group membership are not the same thing.
